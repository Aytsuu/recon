# Problem Statement

Getting help from customer support is harder than it should be.

When a service fails, a charge looks wrong, an order does not arrive, or an
account issue occurs, people must first turn a messy real-world problem into a
clear written request. They need to identify the correct company and support
channel, remember relevant dates and account details, explain what has already
happened, provide the right evidence, and state the outcome they want.

This burden falls especially heavily on people who prefer speaking to typing,
are not confident writing formal support messages, communicate most naturally
in a language other than the provider's support language, or do not understand
the technical vocabulary associated with their problem.

Today's support journeys are fragmented. Information may be spread across
apps, websites, email addresses, chatbots, help-center articles, and phone
menus. Users often do not know which route is appropriate, which details are
essential, or whether their request is complete enough for support to act on it.

The consequence is avoidable delay and frustration:

- Users postpone asking for help or abandon the process altogether.
- Requests are sent to the wrong channel or lack the information needed to
  resolve them.
- Support teams must ask for the same details repeatedly.
- Customers spend time translating, organizing, and rewriting their own
  experience instead of receiving help.
- Important problems can remain unresolved simply because the affected person
  could not easily communicate a complete support case.

The core problem is not only contacting support. It is the gap between a
person's natural account of what happened and the structured, complete,
correctly directed request that a support process expects.
